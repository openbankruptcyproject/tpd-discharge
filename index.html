Total & Permanent Disability (TPD) Discharge

An administrative path to discharge federal student loans for borrowers who have been determined to be totally and permanently disabled. No bankruptcy filing required. Educational reference, not legal advice.

What this discharge is

The Total & Permanent Disability (TPD) Discharge is an administrative program operated by the U.S. Department of Education (and the Veterans Administration for service-connected disabilities). It allows borrowers determined to be totally and permanently disabled to have their federal student loans — Direct Loans, FFEL Program loans, Perkins Loans, and TEACH Grant service obligations — discharged without going through bankruptcy.

TPD is fundamentally different from a § 523(a)(8) bankruptcy AP. There is no court filing, no Brunner test, and no judicial determination. The Department of Education evaluates the disability documentation and grants or denies the discharge administratively.

Eligibility

Three eligibility paths

  1. VA documentation: The Department of Veterans Affairs has determined the borrower is unemployable due to a service-connected disability, or has a 100% service-connected disability rating.
  2. Social Security Administration (SSA) documentation: The borrower receives Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI) or Supplemental Security Income (SSI), with a Medical Improvement Not Expected (MINE) review classification or a 5- to 7-year continuing disability review (CDR) cycle.
  3. Physician certification: A licensed physician (M.D. or D.O.) certifies that the borrower is totally and permanently disabled in the sense that the borrower cannot engage in any substantial gainful activity (SGA) due to a physical or mental impairment that has lasted, or can be expected to last, at least 60 continuous months, or that can be expected to result in death.

Application process

Apply through the Department of Education's TPD discharge servicer (currently Nelnet, as of recent program operation). Submit the application form along with supporting documentation matching one of the three eligibility paths.

If approved, the loan balance is discharged. Historically there was a three-year post-discharge monitoring period during which the discharge could be reversed if the borrower's earnings exceeded a threshold; recent rule changes have eliminated or shortened the monitoring period for most TPD discharges. Borrowers should confirm current rules at the time of application.

How it interacts with bankruptcy

A TPD discharge approval discharges the federal student loan obligation entirely, regardless of any bankruptcy proceeding. Borrowers who qualify for TPD generally do not need to pursue a § 523(a)(8) AP for those same loans.

Private student loans are not covered by TPD — the program is a federal Department of Education program. Private loans require either a § 523(a)(8) AP, a lender-specific disability discharge program (some private lenders offer one voluntarily), or other relief.

Important. This page describes the program in general terms. Eligibility determinations are fact-specific. Talk to a licensed bankruptcy attorney, a legal aid attorney, or a Department of Education ombudsman about your specific situation.
